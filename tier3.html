The Tier 3 model represents the most comprehensive version of the Adaptive Litigation Valuation Framework. Unlike the earlier tiers, which provide static expected-value calculations, Tier 3 models litigation as a dynamic strategic process in which the parties continuously update their decisions as new information becomes available throughout the life of the case.

Overview

We model civil litigation as a two-player, finite-horizon stochastic game with asymmetric information, stage-dependent litigation costs, and endogenous settlement opportunities. The players are the plaintiff and the defendant (or, where applicable, the defendant's liability insurer). Litigation proceeds through a finite sequence of procedural stages, with each player choosing litigation or settlement strategies based on evolving information, expected payoffs, and anticipated future costs.

Planned Analytical Components

  • Finite-horizon stochastic game formulation
  • Sequential bargaining and settlement offers
  • Bayesian updating as new evidence is developed
  • Asymmetric information between litigants
  • Discovery-stage probability revisions
  • Expert witness reliability adjustments
  • Dispositive motion analysis
  • Time-discounted expected recoveries
  • Policy-limit and excess judgment dynamics
  • Appeal probabilities and expected post-trial outcomes
  • Strategic equilibrium analysis

Research Goals

The objective of Tier 3 is to provide a transparent legal-economic framework for evaluating litigation strategy as information evolves over time. Rather than relying on a single expected-value estimate, the model will update settlement recommendations as additional evidence, procedural rulings, expert opinions, and litigation costs become known.

The long-term goal is to provide attorneys with an adaptive decision-support framework capable of integrating traditional law-and-economics analysis, dynamic game theory, and modern large language models into a unified litigation valuation methodology.

Disclaimer

This litigation valuation framework is intended solely for educational, research, and decision-support purposes. The estimates produced by these models are scenario-based calculations derived from user-supplied assumptions and should not be interpreted as predictions or guarantees of litigation outcomes.

Actual case values depend on numerous legal, factual, evidentiary, procedural, and strategic considerations—including witness credibility, judicial rulings, jury determinations, evolving evidence, settlement negotiations, and applicable law—that cannot be fully captured by any mathematical model.

Users should exercise independent professional judgment and verify all inputs, assumptions, and applicable legal standards before relying on these models in practice.
