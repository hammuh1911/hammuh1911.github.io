Overview

The Tier 1 model is designed for rapid preliminary civil litigation valuation using only three primary quantitative inputs. It provides a transparent expected-value estimate and an economically rational settlement range under simplified assumptions. It provides attorneys with an immediate expected-value estimate and a rational settlement range under risk-neutral assumptions. Tier 1 is intended for early case screening, client intake, and preliminary settlement discussions before significant discovery has occurred.

Mathematical Framework

The model assumes rational, risk-neutral parties and estimates the bargaining range by comparing the plaintiff's expected trial recovery with the defendant's expected cost of continued litigation.

Inputs

  • D – Gross Expected Damages
  • Pwin – Net Probability of Prevailing
  • CD – Defense Litigation Costs Through Trial

Plaintiff Expected Trial Verdict


EVPlaintiff = Pwin × D

Expected Defense Cost of Litigation


EVDefendant = (Pwin × D) + CD

Target Settlement Window (ZOPA)


EVPlaintiff ≤ Settlement ≤ min(EVDefendant, L)

where L represents applicable insurance policy limits or other practical collection constraints.

3. Target Settlement Window (ZOPA):
EV_plaintiff to Min(EV_defendant, Policy_Limit)

- Gross Expected Damages (D): [INSERT NUMBER]
- Probability of Win % (P_win): [INSERT %]

### OUTPUT REQUIRED:

1. Executive Summary

2. Mathematical Valuation Breakdown

Disclaimer

This litigation valuation framework is intended solely for educational, research, and decision-support purposes. The estimates produced by these models are scenario-based calculations derived from user-supplied assumptions and should not be interpreted as predictions or guarantees of litigation outcomes.

Actual case values depend on numerous legal, factual, evidentiary, procedural, and strategic considerations—including witness credibility, judicial rulings, jury determinations, evolving evidence, settlement negotiations, and applicable law—that cannot be fully captured by any mathematical model.

Users should exercise independent professional judgment and verify all inputs, assumptions, and applicable legal standards before relying on these models in practice.
