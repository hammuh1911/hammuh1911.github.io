Overview

The Tier 2 model extends the Tier 1 expected value framework by incorporating comparative fault, separate liability and causation probabilities, plaintiff litigation costs, jurisdiction-specific comparative fault rules, and policy limit analysis. It is designed for cases where additional factual development has occurred through investigation or discovery. Tier 2 is intended for cases approaching mediation or advanced settlement negotiations, where liability, damages, and litigation costs can be estimated with greater confidence than at initial intake.

Mathematical Framework

Inputs

  • Decon – Economic damages (medical expenses, lost wages, etc.)
  • Dnon-econ – Non-economic damages (pain and suffering, etc.)
  • CF – Plaintiff comparative fault percentage
  • PL – Probability of prevailing on liability
  • PC – Probability of prevailing on causation
  • CP – Plaintiff litigation costs
  • CD – Defense litigation costs
  • L – Applicable liability insurance policy limit or collectible asset cap

Jurisdiction Comparative Fault Rule

Select the jurisdiction's comparative fault rule.

Pure Comparative Fault

Dadj =
(Decon + Dnon-econ) × (1 − CF)

Modified Comparative Fault (50% Bar)

If CF < 50%

Dadj =
(Decon + Dnon-econ) × (1 − CF)

Otherwise

Dadj = 0

Modified Comparative Fault (51% Bar)

If CF < 51%

Dadj =
(Decon + Dnon-econ) × (1 − CF)

Otherwise

Dadj = 0

Approximate Joint Probability Model

The model approximates the overall probability of success by separately estimating liability and causation. 

Pwin =
PL × PC

Modeling Assumption. The Tier 2 model treats liability and causation as independent events for estimation purposes. Where substantial evidentiary overlap exists, users should interpret this approximation cautiously or substitute a case-specific joint probability estimate.

Core Valuation Equations

Expected Trial Verdict

EVTrial =
Pwin × Dadj

Expected Defense Cost of Litigation

EVDefense =
EVTrial + CD

Plaintiff Reservation Value

Smin =
EVTrial − CP

Defense Reservation Value

Smax =
min(EVDefense, L)

Settlement Window (ZOPA)

Smin ≤ Settlement ≤ Smax

Policy Limit Analysis

If 

EVTrial > L

the model flags potential excess judgment exposure and recommends evaluating the jurisdiction's applicable policy-limit demand and bad-faith rules.
